## The brief

Ardré's modality — FLOWpresso, bodywork, energy work — is felt, not explained. Her existing Wix site couldn't carry that. Pages loaded slow enough that mobile visitors bounced before the homepage finished. Booking pushed people through a flow nobody asked for. And she'd been told that switching meant losing control of her own content.

## Key decisions

**Keep the domain, drop the platform.** `flowdomenz.com` stays. Visitors don't notice the migration. What changes underneath is everything: page weight, mobile speed, how easy it is to edit.

**Stay in control.** The hard line was: *I want to update my own site.* The new editing surface is simpler than Wix but covers the same ground — text, photos, hours, testimonials, blog posts. Changes go live in under a minute. Anything structural is one email away.

**No lock-in.** The site, the content, the domain — all stay hers. If she ever wants to leave, she takes everything with her. There's nothing to extract because it was always already hers.

**Bookings that work.** Wix's booking flow gets replaced with a Stripe-based one connected to her existing account. Cal.com handles scheduling. The path from "I want a session" to a confirmed booking is short enough to use on a phone, one-handed.

## Where it stands

Build is done. Mobile is being finalised. Stripe is wired to her existing account. Go-live is days away, with the domain switch designed for zero downtime.
